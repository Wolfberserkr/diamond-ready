Coaching Notes

Keep It Real

The physical work is only part of the equation. These principles drive results at this age.

🏅
Compete alongside him

The best push you can give isn't instruction — it's competition. When you do the sprints, the push-ups, and the throws together, he works harder naturally. Save the coaching voice for skill blocks.

💪
Protect the arm

He's already doing 30 push-ups daily. Don't double up on upper body volume. Always warm up progressively on throwing days. At 8, the growth plates in the elbow are vulnerable — skip long toss if his arm is tired.

📊
Track two numbers

Sprint time (home to second) and solid hits per 10 swings. Seeing actual improvement in real numbers motivates kids this age more than any pep talk. Let him watch the tracker himself.

🎯
Name the game situation

During Phase 3, call the scenario before every drill. "Runner on second, two outs." Kids who understand their job play with intent instead of just reacting. Baseball IQ is trainable.

😴
Taper weeks 14–15

Dial back conditioning finishers in the final two weeks. Walk into the tournament fresh and confident, not accumulated-tired. Skill work stays sharp — just take the legs off the gas.

🔥
Love of the game first

At 8, love of the game drives everything. Keep energy high. Celebrate contact and good takes equally. The kid who loves baseball at 8 is still playing at 18. The one who burned out isn't.
